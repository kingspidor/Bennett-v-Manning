## I. IDENTITY OF PETITIONER

Zackariah Jeremiah Bennett, pro se and indigent, petitions this Court for review of the Court of Appeals decision affirming summary judgment.

## II. CITATION TO COURT OF APPEALS DECISION

The Court of Appeals, Division One, filed its opinion in No. 87007-6-I on January 26, 2026. The order denying Petitioner's motion for reconsideration was filed on February 18, 2026. Copies of both are attached as Appendices A and B.

## III. ISSUES PRESENTED FOR REVIEW

**Issue 1:** Whether a trial court violates CR 56 and *Young v. Key Pharmaceuticals*, 112 Wn.2d 216 (1989), by granting summary judgment where the moving party's own sworn declaration contains mutually exclusive factual assertions on its face, the nonmoving party filed a sworn opposition with supporting evidence, a motion to compel remained pending because the moving party produced zero discovery responses, and the court denied the nonmoving party's motion to strike the contradictory declaration without findings the day before granting summary judgment.

**Issue 2:** Whether Division One denied Petitioner meaningful access to appellate review by penalizing him for an incomplete record caused by the trial court's failure to comply with Division One's own mandate in *Bennett v. Manning*, No. 84951-4-I (2023), which reversed and remanded for an indigency determination under RAP 15.2(c) that was never conducted.

**Issue 3:** Whether the Court of Appeals violated ER 201(d)'s mandatory judicial notice provision and Petitioner's due process rights by affirming a ruling that found facts directly contradicted by evidence the court itself had admitted into the record.

**Issue 4:** Whether summary judgment based on collateral estoppel can stand where the moving party's counsel took mutually exclusive sworn positions in two different courts within seven days — telling this Court that certain evidence was "not relevant" to the prior proceeding, then telling the Superior Court that the prior proceeding had "adjudicated" those matters — and where the prior court's own record shows it explicitly disclaimed authority over those matters.

## IV. STATEMENT OF THE CASE

### A. Background

Petitioner is the biological father of T.B. (born 2007). In 2008, while Petitioner was incarcerated in Colorado, Respondent Melvina Manning — T.B.'s maternal grandmother — obtained an ex parte custody order. Default judgment was entered without hearing or counsel. The resulting findings contain an internal impossibility: three separate boxes are checked for "abandoned" while a fourth box on the same form states "Both parents threaten to remove child from State of Washington." Appendix C (2008 Findings excerpt). One cannot simultaneously abandon a child and threaten to remove him.

Nine days before those findings were entered, Manning filed a declaration in the same case stating Petitioner was "calling his son on the telephone a couple times per week." This sworn statement — regular telephone contact — is irreconcilable with the "abandonment" finding.

In 2013, Petitioner signed a relinquishment in Snohomish County, induced by a Communication Agreement drafted by attorney Mark M. Demaray. That agreement explicitly stated that continued contact between Petitioner and T.B. was in T.B.'s "best interests," required a judge's signature to be enforceable (§1.1), and required Petitioner to notify both the Court and Manning within 48 hours to withdraw consent. Petitioner was incarcerated — outgoing prison mail takes a minimum of 48 hours to reach even a local recipient, making the withdrawal window physically impossible to exercise. The judge's signature line appears blank. The agreement was not mentioned in or submitted with the relinquishment hearing paperwork, which independently stated the relinquishment was in T.B.'s "best interest." The adoption was never completed; the cause was dismissed in 2019 for lack of progress.

### B. The DVPO Proceeding

In June 2022, Petitioner visited Manning's residence hoping to see T.B. Manning filed a Domestic Violence Protection Order petition. At the August 5, 2022 hearing, the DVPO judge explicitly limited the proceeding's scope:

> "Some of what was filed really does not — is not relevant to the issue that's before me ... the motion to appoint a guardian ad litem, for example, that is not something that this court has the authority to do. That's not part of the protection order statute. So anything that is beyond the scope of why we're here is not going to be something that I want you to focus [on]."

Appendix E (Exhibit B — hearing excerpt). Petitioner confirmed his understanding and informed the court he had refiled those other matters separately. Those refiled matters became Case No. 23-2-11352-7 — the civil action at issue in this petition.

Manning had Ring camera and porch security camera footage of the encounter. Petitioner requested this footage throughout the DVPO proceeding. It was never produced.

### C. The Appellate Mandate — 84951-4-I

Petitioner appealed the DVPO. In *Bennett v. Manning*, No. 84951-4-I (Division One, April 17, 2023), Division One issued a per curiam opinion (Judges Dwyer, Coburn, Andrus) holding that the trial court erred by failing to determine Petitioner's indigency under RAP 15.2(c) and by deciding the ultimate issue of entitlement to public expense review — a question RAP 15.2(c) reserves for this Court. Division One reversed and remanded "for further proceedings consistent with this opinion." Appendix H.

The trial court never complied. Petitioner has resubmitted the mandate; it remains unaddressed.

### D. The Civil Action and Summary Judgment

Petitioner filed civil claims in 23-2-11352-7 — the matters the DVPO court had told him were beyond its scope. Manning, through counsel Nancy Gass (O.W. Hollowell & Associates), filed a Response on September 19, 2023. Manning's sworn declaration contains the following statements:

Page 2: "Mr. Bennett **never made any attempt** to contact TB from 2008 until June 23, 2019."

Page 6: "**one or two letters during the roughly 4 years he was in prison.**"

Appendix C (side-by-side excerpt). These assertions are mutually exclusive within the same sworn document. One cannot have "never made any attempt" to contact a child while also having sent "one or two letters."

During discovery, Gass produced a 93-second cell phone recording of a porch security camera monitor — not the original Ring camera footage. Metadata showed the recording was created on July 9, 2022, nearly a month after the incident. On May 20, 2024, Gass wrote to Petitioner: "Her system deletes videos every 30 days. The only way she knew how to preserve the video was to record it on her cell phone." Appendix F. Two days later, on May 22, 2024, Gass filed a statement with the court claiming Manning had not spoliated evidence and that the video "still exists." Appendix F.

Petitioner filed an opposition to summary judgment with supporting evidence including a 2011 visit video (filmed by Manning herself), contact logs, and correspondence. Exhibit A contains Manning's own acknowledgments of receiving Angel Tree gifts and Read-to-Me-Daddy recordings — prison programs through which incarcerated parents send Christmas gifts and recorded readings to their children — during the very period she swore Petitioner made "no attempt" at contact. Manning also provided Petitioner with the name and contact information for T.B.'s therapist, Rebecca Perbix Mallos, but never gave the therapist permission to speak with Petitioner — the therapist responded that she could not even confirm or deny whether T.B. was a patient. Petitioner filed a Motion to Strike False Statements identifying Manning's internal contradictions. Petitioner's Motion to Compel had been pending since May 6, 2024 — Manning had produced zero discovery responses.

On July 2, 2024, the trial court denied the Motion to Strike and Motion for Sanctions. The order contains no findings. No explanation was provided for why mutually exclusive statements in the same sworn declaration do not create a genuine factual dispute.

On July 3, 2024, the trial court granted summary judgment. The order — drafted by Gass and signed by Judge Hawk — states there are "no genuine issues of material fact." It does not address Petitioner's fraud claims. Discovery remained outstanding.

### E. The Collateral Estoppel Foundation

The summary judgment rests on collateral estoppel. At the June 28, 2024 hearing, Judge Hawk asked Gass for her "strongest legal argument." Gass responded: "All of these issues have been addressed by a court, they've been adjudicated. Mr. Bennett had his opportunity to present this to the court in the domestic violence hearing." Appendix E. The court confirmed this as res judicata and collateral estoppel.

Seven days earlier — on June 21, 2024 — Gass had filed an Answer to Petition for Review in this Court (Case No. 102950-1), stating that the video evidence and civil matters were "not relevant to this case," referring to the DVPO. Appendix D.

The DVPO court's own words confirm Gass's first position was accurate: civil matters were explicitly excluded from the DVPO proceeding. The court said it lacked authority over them. Petitioner refiled them separately. Yet summary judgment was granted on the representation that the DVPO had adjudicated those very matters.

### F. The Appeal

Petitioner appealed. Because the trial court never complied with the mandate in 84951-4-I regarding indigency, Petitioner could not afford a certified transcript. Petitioner included verbatim quotes from the official FTR court recording in his appellate filings, cited to the audio. Manning never challenged the accuracy of these quotes.

The Court of Appeals cited the incomplete record as a basis for declining full de novo review (Opinion p.4, FN 1). On January 26, 2026, Division One affirmed. The opinion found "no contact" between 2008 and 2019 — notwithstanding Exhibit A, which the court had admitted on December 8, 2025 and which documents phone calls, a 2011 video visit, in-person contact, recordings, and gifts throughout that period.

Manning was ordered to respond by December 1, 2025. She filed nothing. Under CR 8(d), all undenied averments are deemed admitted. The court did not address this.

Reconsideration was denied February 18, 2026 without addressing the factual impossibilities Petitioner raised.

## V. ARGUMENT

### A. ISSUE 1: Summary Judgment on a Facially Contradictory Declaration Conflicts with Supreme Court Precedent and Eliminates the CR 56 Standard

**RAP 13.4(b)(1): Conflicts with** *Young v. Key Pharmaceuticals***, 112 Wn.2d 216 (1989), and** *Folsom v. Burger King***, 135 Wn.2d 658 (1998).**
**RAP 13.4(b)(4): Substantial public interest.**

The nonmoving party filed a sworn opposition identifying specific factual disputes supported by documentary evidence. The nonmoving party had a pending Motion to Strike identifying false statements in the moving party's own declaration. The nonmoving party had a pending Motion to Compel because the moving party had produced zero discovery responses in 57 days. The moving party's own sworn declaration contained mutually exclusive assertions on its face — "never made any attempt to contact" on page 2, and "one or two letters" on page 6 of the same filing. The trial court denied the Motion to Strike without findings, then granted summary judgment the following day finding "no genuine issues of material fact."

Under what construction of CR 56 is this permissible?

Under *Young*, the moving party must first demonstrate the absence of a genuine issue of material fact before any burden shifts to the nonmoving party. 112 Wn.2d at 225. Manning's declaration — the foundation of her summary judgment motion — did not demonstrate the absence of a genuine issue. It *created* one. A document that asserts "never" and "one or two" in the same sworn filing does not make a coherent factual assertion from which the absence of dispute can be determined.

Under *Folsom*, all trial court rulings made in conjunction with a summary judgment motion are reviewed de novo. 135 Wn.2d at 663. This includes the July 2, 2024 denial of the Motion to Strike — a ruling issued without findings the day before summary judgment was granted on the very evidence that motion challenged. Under *Keck v. Collins*, all evidence and reasonable inferences must be viewed in the light most favorable to the nonmoving party. 184 Wn.2d at 370.

Washington courts have long recognized that contradictory sworn statements cannot be used to manufacture or eliminate genuine factual disputes. In *Marshall v. AC&S, Inc.*, Division One held that "when a party has given clear answers to unambiguous questions which negate the existence of any genuine issue of material fact, that party cannot thereafter create such an issue with an affidavit that merely contradicts, without explanation, previously given clear testimony." 56 Wn. App. at 185. Under *Berry v. Crown Cork & Seal Co.*, the threshold is whether the statements are "clearly contradictory" as opposed to merely inconsistent. 103 Wn. App. at 321-23. "Never made any attempt" and "one or two letters" are mutually exclusive. They exceed the "clearly contradictory" threshold by any measure.

While these authorities traditionally address contradictions between separate filings, Manning's contradiction is categorically stronger — it occurs within a single document. There is no temporal gap. No intervening discovery. No changed circumstances. The same declaration, signed under the same penalty of perjury, asserts "never" on one page and admits contact on another.

The Eighth Circuit has confirmed that scrutiny of contradictory sworn statements applies to the moving party's own declarations supporting summary judgment. *Button v. Dakota, Minn. & E. R.R. Corp.*, 963 F.3d 824 (8th Cir. 2020). But this Court need not extend any doctrine. The issue is simpler: under *Young*, how does a facially self-contradicting declaration demonstrate the absence of a genuine issue of material fact?

The procedural context compounds the error. The trial court denied the Motion to Strike without findings on July 2, then granted summary judgment on July 3 — based on the declarations that motion challenged. A Motion to Compel had been pending for 57 days because Manning produced zero discovery responses. Under *Folsom*, every one of these rulings is subject to de novo review. The Court of Appeals affirmed without addressing the facial contradiction in Manning's declaration or the absence of findings on the Motion to Strike.

This presents a question of substantial public interest under RAP 13.4(b)(4). If a moving party can obtain summary judgment while her own declaration contradicts itself on its face, and the trial court can deny evidentiary challenges without findings the day before granting that motion, the CR 56 standard is a dead letter. Every litigant who files a meritorious opposition — particularly pro se and indigent litigants without counsel to escalate procedural violations in real time — is at risk.

### B. ISSUE 2: Division One's Mandate Was Ignored, Then Its Consequences Were Held Against Petitioner

**RAP 13.4(b)(3): Significant constitutional question — access to courts.**
**RAP 13.4(b)(4): Substantial public interest.**

In April 2023, Division One issued a per curiam opinion holding that the trial court erred by failing to determine Petitioner's indigency under RAP 15.2(c) and by deciding the question of entitlement to public expense review that RAP 15.2(c) reserves for this Court. Division One reversed and remanded with instructions. Appendix H.

The trial court never complied. Petitioner resubmitted the mandate. It remains unaddressed.

In January 2026, the same Division One cited the resulting incomplete record — the absence of a certified transcript Petitioner could not afford because the trial court refused to fund it — as the basis for declining to conduct full de novo review. Opinion at 4, FN 1.

The logic is circular. An appellate court reversed and remanded so Petitioner could obtain transcripts through an indigency determination. The trial court ignored the mandate. The appellate court then penalized Petitioner for lacking the transcripts the mandate was supposed to produce.

This raises a significant constitutional question under RAP 13.4(b)(3). If appellate reversal and remand orders carry no enforcement mechanism — if a trial court can ignore an appellate mandate without consequence and the appellate court then treats the resulting harm as the petitioner's burden — appellate review is illusory. The right of access to courts guaranteed by the Washington Constitution, Art. I, § 10, is meaningless if the remedy obtained through that access can be nullified by noncompliance.

This also presents an issue of substantial public interest under RAP 13.4(b)(4). Every indigent litigant who obtains a favorable appellate ruling on indigency is at risk if trial courts can decline to implement those rulings without sanction. The three-layer trap here — appellate court orders relief, trial court ignores order, appellate court penalizes petitioner for the resulting gap — would apply to any litigant who depends on court-funded resources to perfect an appeal.

### C. ISSUE 3: Mandatory Judicial Notice Ignored; Admitted Evidence Treated as Nonexistent

**RAP 13.4(b)(3): Significant constitutional question.**
**RAP 13.4(b)(4): Substantial public interest.**

ER 201(d) provides that a court "shall take judicial notice if requested by a party and supplied with the necessary information." "Shall" is mandatory in Washington statutory construction. Petitioner filed nine requests for judicial notice of court records across these proceedings. All were ignored — no rulings, no findings, no denials.

More critically, the Court of Appeals admitted Exhibit A into evidence on December 8, 2025. Exhibit A documents contact between Petitioner and T.B. throughout the period 2008-2019 — phone calls, a 2011 video visit Manning herself filmed, in-person contact, recordings, and gifts. Seven weeks later, the January 26, 2026 opinion found "no contact" during that period as though Exhibit A did not exist.

Additionally, the court ordered Manning to respond by December 1, 2025. Manning filed nothing. Under CR 8(d), undenied averments are deemed admitted. The court did not address this.

A court that admits evidence and then issues a ruling directly contradicted by that evidence has denied the submitting party any meaningful opportunity to be heard. This raises a significant constitutional question under RAP 13.4(b)(3). If "shall" does not mean "shall" in ER 201(d), and if admitted evidence can be disregarded in the ruling it was admitted to inform, every litigant relying on judicial notice or evidentiary submissions is at risk.

### D. ISSUE 4: Collateral Estoppel Built on Mutually Exclusive Sworn Positions

**RAP 13.4(b)(1): Conflicts with** *Arkison v. Ethan Allen, Inc.***, 160 Wn.2d 535 (2007).**
**RAP 13.4(b)(4): Substantial public interest.**

Summary judgment rested on collateral estoppel — the argument that the DVPO proceeding had adjudicated Petitioner's civil claims. Three independent sources establish that this argument was false.

First, the DVPO judge's own words. At the August 5, 2022 hearing, the court stated that civil matters were "not relevant to the issue that's before me," that the court did not have "the authority" to address them, and that "anything that is beyond the scope of why we're here is not going to be something that I want you to focus [on]." Petitioner confirmed his understanding and told the court he had refiled those matters separately.

Second, Gass's own prior position. On June 21, 2024, Gass filed an Answer in this Court stating that the video evidence and civil matters were "not relevant to this case" — referring to the DVPO. This is consistent with what the DVPO judge actually said.

Third, Gass's contradictory subsequent position. Seven days later, on June 28, 2024, Gass told Judge Hawk that the DVPO had "adjudicated" all civil issues — directly contradicting both the DVPO judge's stated limitation and Gass's own filing to this Court one week prior.

Under *Arkison*, judicial estoppel prevents a party from prevailing on a position clearly inconsistent with a position successfully maintained in an earlier proceeding. 160 Wn.2d at 538. The elements are satisfied: (1) "not relevant to the DVPO" and "the DVPO adjudicated everything" are clearly inconsistent — they are mutually exclusive; (2) acceptance of the second position creates the perception that either this Court or the Superior Court was misled; and (3) Manning derived the ultimate advantage — summary judgment — from the second position.

The pattern extends beyond the 7-day contradiction. On May 20, 2024, Gass admitted to Petitioner in writing that Manning's Ring camera footage had been destroyed — "her system deletes videos every 30 days." Two days later, Gass filed a statement with the court claiming the video was not spoliated and "still exists." The method is consistent: one statement to the opposing party, the opposite to the court.

The net effect is a closed evidentiary loop. The video of the triggering event cannot enter through the DVPO because Gass told this Court it was "not relevant" there. The civil claims arising from that event cannot proceed because Gass told the Superior Court the DVPO resolved them. The original footage was destroyed. Evidence that disproves the foundation of summary judgment has no courtroom left to enter.

If this Court does not address counsel's ability to take opposite positions in different courts within seven days and prevail on the second position unchallenged, the integrity of the adversarial system is at stake. This warrants review under both RAP 13.4(b)(1) — conflict with *Arkison* — and RAP 13.4(b)(4) — substantial public interest.
